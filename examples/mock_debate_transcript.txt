MODERATOR: Welcome to the Riverside mayoral debate. First question, to Mayor Reyes. Housing costs rose eleven percent last year. What specifically will you do about it?
REYES: Thank you. Look, everybody knows this city is the greatest city in the country, and nobody loves it more than I do. The other side wants to destroy everything we built together. People are scared, and they should be scared. I will fight for you every single day.
MODERATOR: Councilor Park, same question. What will you do about housing costs?
PARK: We will permit four thousand new homes near transit by 2028. My plan cuts approval times from eighteen months to six. We will pay for it by ending the parking subsidy downtown, which costs twelve million dollars a year. That is the plan, and it is on my website.
MODERATOR: Mayor Reyes, your response.
REYES: She talks about plans, but plans are just words. I have been doing this for a long time, and frankly I have the best record anyone has ever seen. Crime is way down. Actually, crime was never my focus, it is the economy. And the economy has never been stronger.
MODERATOR: Councilor Park, the mayor says crime is down. Is that true?
PARK: Police department data shows burglaries fell four percent, but car thefts rose nine percent. So it depends on the category. I would rather talk about the numbers than about who loves the city more.
MODERATOR: Let's turn to transit. Mayor Reyes, the Line 3 extension is two years late and forty million dollars over budget. Who is responsible?
REYES: That is a great question, and I am glad you asked it. Transit matters to every family in this city. We have incredible workers, the best workers, and I thank them. The real question is why my opponent voted against the budget. Families deserve better than her politics.
MODERATOR: Councilor Park, did you vote against the transit budget?
PARK: I voted against one amendment that moved money from bus service to parking garages. I voted for the final budget. The council record from March shows both votes. I think voters can read it themselves.
MODERATOR: Final question. Mayor Reyes, you said in your first answer that you would fight every day. Will you commit to a public housing target tonight?
REYES: I never said I would set targets. What I said is that targets are what failing politicians use when they have nothing else. We are winning, and everyone can feel it. Believe me, the best is yet to come for this city.
MODERATOR: Councilor Park, the same question. Will you commit to a target?
PARK: Yes. Four thousand permitted homes by the end of 2028, and I will publish progress every quarter. If we miss it, you should hold me to it.
MODERATOR: Thank you both. That concludes tonight's debate.
